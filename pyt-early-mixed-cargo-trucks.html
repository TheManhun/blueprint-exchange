What it does

Grain, fuel, tools and planks — in the same truck, on the same trip. Four early trucks from 1890 to 1985, each with four cargo slots, and every slot takes bulk, liquid, goods or flatbed cargo. One truck, one trip, a mixed load.

▶ Get the whole set in one click: PYT - Mixed Cargo Collection — the Early and Late trucks, the Vanilla Conversion Kit (59 base-game vehicles) and Load Planner.

The game's trucks carry one kind of cargo at a time: a tanker for fuel, a tipper for grain, a flatbed for planks. Feeding a town that wants four things means four lines, or one truck that waits around. These trucks just load what's waiting — up to four kinds at once — and drop each one where it's wanted.

Why is Load Manager included?

Here's a little secret about these trucks. 😄

I originally wanted an easier way to manage cargo loading on trains and other vehicles. Then I had a thought: why can't trucks carry a proper mix of cargo too?

So we built the mixed-cargo trucks!

The funny thing is, Load Manager was the idea that inspired them in the first place. The trucks made it out into the world first, but the loader was always the reason behind them.

So it only seems fair to package them together. Now you can experience the trucks the way I originally imagined them.

Four cargo types. One truck. Precise loading at every stop.

And if you prefer the original loading controls, you can switch Load Manager off in the mod settings.

Enjoy!

How it works

  • Four slots per truck. Each slot takes bulk, liquid, goods or flatbed cargo — the truck's window shows all four specializations.
  • Set the mix on each stop's Load panel — the game's own panel. Pick the cargos, give each a share, and the truck fills its slots to match.
  • Worth buying as a plain truck too: fair, vanilla-style speed, power and capacity for its era. The mixed load is the bonus.

Latest update

1.4 — WORN AND WEATHERED

Four trucks went out in the rain for a few decades. They came back looking like they had earned it.

In plain terms: All four trucks get a worn, weathered finish, rims a clear shade lighter than the tyres, nothing floating at any distance, lighter far-away models, and new shop pictures; the built-in Load Manager gets a Close button on console.

  • New: a worn finish on all four trucks — faded paint, road dirt and weathered wood
  • New: shop pictures in the game's own style — close up from the front, lamps lit, a driver at the wheel
  • Better: rims a clear shade lighter than the tyres on every truck
  • Fixed: nothing floating at any distance — the Dropside's axle, hinges and tank hangers, the Eight-Wheeler's springs and door plates, the Lorry's plates and boards
  • Better: lighter models far away, so a busy road costs less to draw
  • Fixed: Load Manager: on console its windows could not be closed without reloading the save (thanks Maxmadimus) — a Close button, bottom right
  • Fixed: Load Manager: a line of Vanilla Kit ships or aircraft was read as a truck line

What it does

"It might be a mini map, but it’s packed with information."

Towns, roads, rail, stations, industries, every line, your depots and their maintenance reach — in one window. Click anything and the camera flies there.

Where was that coal mine? Which towns does my bus line actually reach? Is anything maintaining the trucks out east? The Minimap answers at a glance: your map from above, north always up, an arrow where your camera is — and every layer a click on or off.

Deutsch? Eine Kurzfassung auf Deutsch findet ihr weiter unten.

What you get

  • Know your towns — every town by name, its population and where its land ends; the capital in gold
  • Find the factory — every industry by type, or Only the ones I ship from: your own supply chain
  • Find that item — every product as its own icon on the map; click one to fly straight there
  • Find your stations — train stations, bus and truck stops, harbours, airports and drone ports, each in its own colour (colour-blind safe)
  • Find your depots — train, road, tram and ship depots, aircraft hangars and maintenance buildings
  • Check your maintenance coverage — the reach of every depot and maintenance building, from the game’s own figures; road, rail, ships and aircraft each with its own switch, the busy ones in orange
  • Follow every line — each in its colour along the real roads and rails; lines that can’t run all the way are flagged, and any line opens in the game’s line manager from the list
  • Spot the traffic jams — jams in orange, your own roads in yellow, every road type in its colour, and where tram track ends
  • Plan a path — sketch your next line across the map, name and colour it, and build along it; your sketches are saved with your game
  • Three sizes — S, M and L, the side panel folds away, and the map reads itself again about once a minute

How to start

  1. The PYT row at the top of the screen > the map tile
  2. It opens with roads and town names; click a heading icon to show stations, industries, lines, depots and more — the panel on the right filters each one
  3. Click anything on the map to fly there

Latest update

1.5 — MADE TO MEASURE

Ten66 asked why their long, thin map came in a square box with dark curtains drawn either side. We took the curtains down.

In plain terms: on a 1:2 or 1:3 map the minimap now takes the map's own shape -- no dark borders, and the map shown bigger -- and the window follows it.

  • New: a map that isn't square fills the minimap — its short side at full size, its long side running on. No more dark borders, and a 1:2 or 1:3 map is shown up to two or three times as big
  • New: the window follows the map's shape — on a narrow map the buttons wrap into extra rows, the layer icons get a little smaller and the title shortens to MM. S, M and L keep their sizes, and the window always stays on the screen
  • Better: the window no longer hops when you pick a size or fold it — it only moves when it would run off the screen
  • A square map looks just as before
  • Thanks: to Ten66 for the idea

What it does

A whole storage camp with its own store for every cargo on your map — and the stock piles up where you can see it.

A normal warehouse store takes whichever cargo of its kind turns up first — one crate of the wrong thing and it is blocked until it empties. The Mega Warehouse locks every store to one cargo: Bricks go in the Bricks store, Fish in the Fish store, and nothing else gets in their way.

What you get

  • One locked store per cargo — every cargo your map has, each with its name on its board. Cargos from other mods join in their own kind
  • The stock on show — liquids fill riveted tanks, bulk piles up in bunkers, crates and pallets fill the cold store and every floor of the three-floor rack hall, timber and steel stack in the yard
  • Sizes from 500 to 5,000 — 500, 1,000, 2,000 or 5,000 per cargo, in a Compact or Classic layout. The camp grows to match, and so do the price and the upkeep
  • Your space, your way — in its window, slide space between cargos of the same kind (Liquid, Bulk, Flatbed, Goods), Lock the ones you want kept, Balance the rest, then Apply. A store never drops below what it holds
  • Every Transport Fever 3 language — the boards and the window follow your game's language, all 13
  • The game's own look — the same cargo icons and colour chips as every other window
  • Bring your own stations — no truck bays built in: put your truck stops, rail, harbour or airport beside it, the way you want
  • 1900 style — brick, slate and timber. More eras are on the workbench for a later edition
  • Needs no other mod

How to start

  1. Warehouses menu > PYT tab > Mega Warehouse — or hover the PYT icon at the top of the screen and click the warehouse tile
  2. Pick the size: Each cargo 500, 1,000, 2,000 or 5,000
  3. Place it and build your stations beside it — click it any time to see every store and share out the space

Good to know

  • It is big: the 5,000 camp is a whole district. Start small — the 500 camp suits most networks
  • The split works inside one kind: you can't store liquid on shelves
  • Each store's size is its maximum stock: slide Canned Food down to 250 and once it holds 250, trucks stop unloading Canned Food there
  • When do I get paid? Not when cargo goes INTO the warehouse — that's Transport Fever 3's rule for every warehouse (in TF2 you were paid for that leg; in TF3 you're not). You're paid when the cargo reaches its customer, a town or a factory that uses it, and the income is then shared, by distance, between every line that carried it. So a train filling the warehouse shows only its running costs until the trucks deliver the stock on — then it gets its share

Latest update

2.4 — YOUR GAME, YOUR RULES

JamesJustice told us a Mega Warehouse was so cheap it felt like cheating. He was right. Now you choose how much it hurts.

In plain terms: three new settings in the mod setup let hardcore players make warehouses expensive to build and to run; left alone, nothing changes.

  • New: three settings in the mod setup, before a game loads — Build cost (the game's price, or 2, 3, 5 or 10 times it: the build menu, placing, Grow and Upgrade all charge it), Running cost (the game's upkeep, or $25k to $300k a year per 500 of capacity per cargo, replacing it — booked once a game month under Road maintenance) and Extra cost when full (0 to 200% more for a full camp)
  • New: the sliders show the prices for a 500 camp as you move them, with a short chart underneath; each warehouse's window shows its own running cost and a Size / Buy / Min / Max chart for your settings
  • Tip: the running cost follows the camp's size (its whole capacity) and, with the extra-when-full setting, how full it is right now — a lean camp pays less each month, and a smaller camp costs less
  • Left at Game / Game / 0%, the warehouse costs exactly what it did
  • Better: the alternative-bays keeper waits for a rebuild to finish before it looks at the lines
  • Thanks: to JamesJustice for the idea
